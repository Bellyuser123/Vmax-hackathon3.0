⚖️ Judicial Cognizance & Sanction Scrutiny Checklist

Statutory checklist for judicial officers evaluating corruption FIRs under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 & BNSS 2023.

🏛️ 1. Territorial & Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
Special Court
The Special Judge for CBI cases at Bhubaneswar is vested with jurisdiction under the DSPE Act for central agency investigations within Odisha. Offence location (EMRS Nuapada) is within the supervisory purview.
👤 2. Public Servant Classification (§2(c) PC Act)
Accused Status
Accused Shri C. Krishna Murthy Naidu serves as Principal in an Eklavya Model Residential School (EMRS), a government-funded institution under the Ministry of Tribal Affairs / State Society, satisfying Section 2(c) public servant criteria.
🛡️ 3. Section 17A PC Act Approval vs. Trap Proviso
Pre-Inquiry Rule
Section 17A mandates prior approval for inquiry into official recommendations. However, the proviso clarifies that where the allegation involves acceptance of illegal gratification or on-the-spot trap, no prior approval is required. Independent verification by SI Ayan Mishra satisfies threshold scrutiny.
📜 4. Section 19 Sanction for Judicial Cognizance
Cognizance Stage
Sanction for prosecution under Section 19 is mandatory before taking formal cognizance upon submission of the final charge-sheet under Section 193 BNSS. It is NOT required at the FIR registration or remand monitoring stage.
⚖️ 5. Pre-Trial Custody & Remand Factors (§187 BNSS)
Remand Review
Section 7 PC Act carries 3 to 7 years imprisonment. If custodial arrest is made, the Court scrutinizes: (i) risk of tampering with subordinate staff records, (ii) necessity of trap recovery verification, and (iii) compliance with Section 35(3) BNSS written arrest reasons.
